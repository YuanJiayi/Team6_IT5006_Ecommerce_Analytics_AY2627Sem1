# Staged late-delivery audit: tables and caveats

All values below are generated by `experiments/phase2_staged_audit.py` with seed 42. New test comparisons are post hoc diagnostics. `AP/rate` is average precision divided by the late rate of that same cohort. `P/R/L@k` means precision, recall, and lift among the highest-scored k% of orders. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals from 1,000 resamples.

Univariate directions were fixed from the feature meanings: shorter promise and fewer days left, and longer distance, route time, seller handover time or current handover delay indicate higher risk; same-state is treated as lower risk. No direction was reversed after seeing the test.

The fixed rule, chosen without fitting weights, is: route typical days + seller past handover days + distance/500 − promised days − same-state indicator; at Stage 2 it also adds handover-after-approval days and subtracts days left at handover. Positive scores mean higher risk. Compare its rows with the full logistic rows in Section 1.

Caveat: these directions and coefficients are exploratory; the test has already been opened. A stronger test result cannot be used to replace the frozen model.

## Anything that surprised me

- Checkout pooled test ROC-AUC is 0.668, but within-month pair-weighted ROC-AUC for June–August is 0.582; August alone is 0.522.

- A fixed, hand-written checkout score gets test ROC-AUC 0.784 versus 0.668 for the frozen logistic model, although its validation mean ROC-AUC is 0.625 versus 0.677. This is a post-hoc comparison, not a new selection.

- Removing the full promise bundle changes checkout test ROC-AUC from 0.668 to 0.463 and Stage 2 from 0.805 to 0.626. Removing only `promised_days` barely changes either score because promise-derived inputs remain.

- Stage 2 test PR-AUC is 0.208; after excluding 120 handovers after the promised date it is 0.157 on 19,110 orders. Those 120 were all late.

- The previous staged validation experiment used purchase-to-handover delay and excluded post-promise handovers. This audit uses the requested approval-to-handover delay and reports the full eligible handover cohort, so those older results are not directly comparable.
