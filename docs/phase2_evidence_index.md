# Phase 2 evidence index — 9 October 2026

The [joint report PDF](../reports/Team6_Phase2_IT5006_AY2627Sem1.pdf) and [editable source](../reports/phase2_joint_report.tex) tell one delivery-reliability story: review approved orders using a score based on purchase-time inputs, then update the time estimate at carrier handover. The original model choices and their later-period tests remain identifiable. Experiments prompted by opened test results are reported as retrospective diagnostics, even when their later-period numbers look better.

## Completed comparisons

| Question | Result and evidentiary status | Reproducible source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does an operational checkout rule beat the model? | Historical route days minus promised days loses to logistic in all five original validation windows (mean PR-AUC 0.127 vs 0.200), but wins in the opened later period (0.124 vs 0.065). Test result is post hoc. | [Consolidation script](../experiments/phase2_consolidation.py), [`checkout_rule.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/checkout_rule.csv) |
| Should the model fit on a shorter period? | Removing sparse 2016 orders barely changes validation. A trailing 180-day fit improves five-window validation PR-AUC (0.2080 vs 0.2003) and MAE (5.678 vs 5.810 days), but worsens both later approved-order scores (0.0613 vs 0.0651; 5.520 vs 5.005 days). It does not replace the all-eligible fitting horizon. This changes training rows, not the time window for a historical feature. | [Training-window audit](../experiments/phase2_training_window_audit.py), [validation summary](../experiments/phase2_training_window_audit/summary.csv), [later comparison](../experiments/phase2_training_window_audit/later_comparison.csv) |
| Where are classification and regression errors concentrated? | The global top-10% list misses 334 of 393 August late orders and all 288 late orders on routes at most 100 km; ridge test MAE rises with promise length and distance. Descriptive post-hoc slices, not subgroup policy selection. | [`classification_error_slices.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/classification_error_slices.csv), [`regression_error_slices.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/regression_error_slices.csv) |
| Does a predicted delivery-versus-promise gap classify lateness? | Version 2 logistic on an inner time-split regression gap has mean validation PR-AUC 0.136 and opened-test PR-AUC 0.124. Its pre-specified monthly refit scored 0.111 on the opened test. Its design followed the original classification test and does not replace that result. | [`classification_v2.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/classification_v2.csv), [pre-regression-test design](phase2_regression_spec.md) |
| Does route-weighted least squares improve checkout regression? | Mean validation MAE 5.796 versus 5.810 for unweighted ridge: a small exploratory gain. | [`wls_validation.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/wls_validation.csv) |
| Does the checkout classifier require the recorded promise? | Removing promised days and derived slack reduces mean validation PR-AUC from 0.200 to 0.175; opened-test PR-AUC falls to 0.033. The original checkout availability assumption is material. | [`promise_availability.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/promise_availability.csv) |
| Do clipping or redundant-slack removal matter? | Clipping 6 negative validation predictions and 10 negative test predictions changes MAE by less than 0.001 day. Dropping promise slack gives validation MAE 5.809 versus 5.810 and makes the coefficient table less redundant. | [`prediction_clip.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/prediction_clip.csv), [`drop_slack_validation.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/drop_slack_validation.csv), [final coefficients](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/regression_final_coefficients_without_slack.csv) |
| Can a 90th-percentile model set the promised date? | This separate promise-setting decision is summarised in the report's exploratory-path appendix. Neither the regularised linear nor shallow boosted candidate beats Olist's recorded promise on mean validation 90th-percentile pinball loss or reaches 90% calendar-date coverage. The boosted candidate improves later-period pinball loss. | [`promise_quantile.csv`](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/promise_quantile.csv), [prediction rows](../experiments/phase2_consolidation/promise_quantile_predictions.csv) |
| Does recent route history help? | A point-in-time 90-day carrier-leg history reduces checkout ridge mean validation MAE from 5.810 to 5.679, better in all five windows. A 30-day total-route history also helps, so the carrier split is not a unique explanation. | [Recent-history audit](../experiments/phase2_recent_history_audit/README.md) |
| Can handover update the estimate? | On the same eligible cohorts, elapsed seller time plus 90-day route carrier history reduces mean validation MAE from 5.809 to 5.304; the opened-test comparison is 4.991 vs 3.762 on 19,230 orders. It is a later prediction, not a checkout score. | [Handover table](../experiments/phase2_recent_history_audit/handover.csv) |
| Do promise regime, marketplace climate, label semantics or later stage information explain results? | Each was tested. The regime and climate additions lower mean validation classification performance; a strict timestamp target changes 347 same-date test labels; staged predictions use distinct later cohorts. | [Promise regime](../experiments/phase2_promise_regime_audit/scores.csv), [climate](../experiments/phase2_marketplace_climate_audit/scores.csv), [label audit](../experiments/phase2_label_definition_audit/frozen_scores.csv), [stage audit](../experiments/phase2_staged_audit/TABLES.md) |

The [consolidation script](../experiments/phase2_consolidation.py) writes its tables under `experiments/phase2_consolidation/` without modifying frozen selection artifacts. Run it from the repository root with `it5006-proj/bin/python experiments/phase2_consolidation.py`. It fits the additional quantile models and takes several minutes. Its calendar-date check asserts that Olist's recorded promise coverage matches the saved lateness label, including same-date deliveries. The remaining scripts have run instructions in their headers or linked READMEs.

The source CSVs do not identify the operational cause of the 2018 delivery and promise shift, the effect or cost of staff intervention, or the exact historical visibility of stored promise and payment values at checkout. Those are limits of these completed retrospective analyses, not unrun model experiments.
